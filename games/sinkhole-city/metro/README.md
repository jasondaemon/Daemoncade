## Infinity: continuous scale (`finale47`)

Completion now means the board is empty, not merely that the size threshold was reached. Journey progress is capped at 99% while matter remains. Consuming the last galactic-scale specimen starts a 4.5-second committed final intake: remaining specimens spiral/shrink into the opening and are credited only when that animation finishes. All objects must actually be consumed before 100%, the final mission medal and the complete result. Earlier Matter/Life milestones retain their continuous-growth objectives. The last mission uses rules-v4 while retaining previous medals and separating timing records. The galactic HUD counts remaining specimens; decorative stars fade through interstellar scale, and terrain/sky become black at galactic scale. At the end no props, star field or lit hole rim remains. Simulation-clock pause/resume also freezes this final intake.

Added a sixth campaign world, unlocked after completing Solar Collapse (the existing three-Orbit-medal gate also applies). Three sequential missions end after scales 2, 11 and 18; Free Roam and same-run continuation can traverse the full sequence. There are eighteen contracts and 54 possible medals. Existing saves, earlier medals/unlocks, ten rim rewards and Arena records remain compatible; the extra world does not reset old progress. The six-world selection fits the existing phone/landscape layouts.

The eighteen authored scales are Atoms → Molecules → Living cells → Microfauna → Leaf litter → Garden → Undergrowth → Street life → Neighborhood → City → Landscapes → Regions → Archipelagos → Continents → Planets → Stars → Star clouds → Galaxies. Added intermediate specimens include diatoms, rotifers, tardigrades, moss, pebbles, leaves, grasses, ferns, mushrooms, shrubs, saplings, people, bicycles, cars, sheds, apartment/office buildings, city blocks, valleys, plateaus, lakes, landmasses, ice caps and star clusters. Houses are now single buildings rather than a normalized three-house miniature. These are playful illustrations, not scientifically literal diagrams or physical scale ratios. Planet surfaces reuse the local direction-space coloring with lower tessellation; no new external assets are required.

The discrete-board prototype was rejected: loading a new stage and resetting the hole destroyed the growth illusion. Infinity now has 1,224 seeded objects in one shared coordinate space. Each size range has a different jittered distribution. Objects from larger ranges are visible before they fit; smaller uncollected objects shrink/dissolve out of relevance without granting score or becoming consumed. Radius, position and velocity never reset at a size boundary. The camera's display units continuously track growth; palette interpolation is continuous too. The labels describe continuous logarithmic growth, not board loads. Ratios remain compressed for play, but hierarchy tests require houses to dwarf insects and prevent mountains and ants from sharing a visible range. Mountains now have regional/continental steps before planets.

Collection now uses a committed spiral intake for these illustrative floating forms, rather than making enormous molecular structures fall under unscaled terrestrial gravity. Electron orbits use per-part vertex animation; forms drift/rotate, cells breathe, and galaxies rotate. Animation uses simulation time and freezes when paused. Reduced motion disables ornamental rotation/orbit animation. Molecular bonds now connect their actual nodes, DNA has paired backbones, cells have membrane lips/mitochondria, and garden/galaxy models have additional structure. This remains stylized art, not a biological or astronomical simulation.

A bounded model-source set persists for a run; no geometry or ground is rebuilt at scale boundaries. Per-frame instance compaction omits sub-visible/distant/consumed objects from GPU drawing. Unit routes cover three seeds × three contracts and same-run continuation, including delayed consumption of tiny remnants during the finale. The native fixture checks all eighteen size contexts, overlapping ranges, density, shader compilation, paused/moving frames, bounded resource residency, phone layouts and the empty ending. The larger journey has revised mission targets/deadlines: previous medals survive without comparing unlike timing records (Matter/Life rules-v3, Everything rules-v4). Exploration ranks remain bounded for save compatibility despite the larger coordinate range. Human pacing, art acceptance and real-phone FPS/thermal qualification remain open. Public beta/install routing is unchanged.

Each world now has a continuous, size-driven camera profile rather than the old shared `35 + radius × 2.6` distance. The starting view is closer so early targets are legible; later growth reveals more of the world while the hole still grows on screen. The lens stays at 40°: this is a smooth dolly, not an animated field-of-view distortion.

All craft/planets follow continuous, simulation-timed orbits. Planets have ocean/land, desert bands, tilted rings or icy bands; spacecraft have panel cells, dishes, cockpits and engine details. A static world-space procedural starfield/nebula and subtle orbit guides add context without external images/models, particle storms or extra scene lights. This is a stylized miniature orbital system, not realistic astrophysical scale.

Orbiters use a separate gravity-well capture path, starting at their actual altitude/velocity rather than a pavement rigid body. Shallow abandoned catches recover toward their orbit at bounded speed; committed catches retain their intake even if the player leaves. Points/growth wait until the full conservative tilted envelope is submerged. Oversized objects cannot be captured. The star is sealed until all four planets are fully swallowed, and its release event occurs exactly once. The three contracts cover 45 fragments, 12 intercepted spacecraft, and the four-planets→star finale. Clearing the last contract with every earlier contract recorded shows an Expedition Complete result; revisiting worlds remains available.

The five-world campaign now offers **15 contracts / 45 medals** with unchanged save keys/backup format and Metro Arena rules. Five world tabs fit a compact two-row selector. `orbit.test.mjs` covers progression, saves, bounded orbital motion, fixed-clock frame-rate equivalence, shallow recovery/deep capture and continuous completion routes. `scripts/test-sinkhole-orbit.mjs` checks cold asset isolation, real model routes, live motion, pause, persistence/completion, locked/unlocked mobile decks and repeated five-world resource cleanup. ## Traffic and collection feedback

Metro Expedition and Free Roam now contain four moving cars, bringing the district to 206 collectibles. They follow a continuous, constant-speed rounded street loop, remain spaced apart and can be intercepted once the hole is large enough. A caught car transfers its travel momentum to the contact solver, then stays off its old route; it never teleports back into traffic after a shallow recovery. Moving vehicles count toward the existing vehicle contract. Map arrows distinguish traffic from stationary props. This is an authored traffic loop, not a general road-network/inter-vehicle collision simulation.

Arena deliberately retains its original 202-object stationary layout and rules-v1 records; traffic does not silently change its competitive conditions.

Collection awards now appear briefly at the object position, using a fixed eight-label pool. Low Effects disables these labels, and reduced motion suppresses their rise. Material-specific procedural sounds layer filtered noise with damped, inharmonic resonances for metal, wood, soft props and masonry. A compressor, conservative master gain and eight-voice limit (four in Low Effects) bound the mix. Pause, mute, app suspension and run completion stop/disconnect every active source; resuming does not replay a sound backlog. No external audio assets were added. The final mix still needs headphone/speaker and real-phone listening checks.

## Metro Arena prototype

Select **Arena** from the mission deck. Matches run for up to two minutes (or until the district is cleared), with three explicitly labeled AI rivals. All four holes use the same movement, starting radius, contact solver, growth and scoring rules. Easy introduces decision pauses; Normal plans toward larger objects; Hard reconsiders growth-oriented routes more frequently. Spawn corners are separated, but district symmetry and human difficulty balance are not yet accepted release gates.

The city is shared: contact claims prevent competing holes from awarding the same prop twice. Shallow objects that settle can be released again; committed falls retain their owner. All four openings cut the actual ground and have depth walls and colored rims. Rivals have map markers and on-screen AI labels. Live rank handles ties; results show all four scores. Best scores and match/win counts are stored separately per difficulty under Arena rules version 1, included in save backups and isolated from campaign medals. Existing pre-Arena Metro backups remain importable. Pausing suspends the entire match, clears steering and stops pending sound sources instead of replaying old effects on resume.

This is a **resource-race Arena**, not online multiplayer, and holes do not attack/eat one another. Arena outside Metro, hole combat if adopted, human-tested balancing, richer audio and sustained mobile performance qualification are still pending. A normal collection route can win Easy with actual loaded model dimensions; an idle player loses to active rivals in all three difficulty fixtures. These are regression checks, not a substitute for human playtests.

The rim-contact pass replaces the shared fall animation and player attachment. A sampled base determines loss of support; shallow grazes use a damped rocking response. Eligible objects losing sufficient support activate a 120 Hz body solver with dimensions taken from the loaded model, angular inertia, gravity, pavement/inner-wall contacts, friction and modest restitution. A mild downward pressure/curl field keeps small clutter collectable while moving. Bodies can settle back onto the street when the opening moves away, or bridge an edge until the opening is repositioned/enlarged. Scoring waits until the body is fully below street level. Local rim impacts generate bounded debris and short material-dependent noise effects. These are simplified box/contact proxies, not full mesh collision or inter-object rigid-body physics; hollow structures and unusual silhouettes need further refinement.

Deep falls now have a one-way intake state. Once the center lies well below street level, closing ground no longer applies collision impulses to that body. It finishes under gravity at its world-space intake, preserving rotation and horizontal momentum; its upper collision envelope cannot rise back through the street. Shallow catches can still recover before this point. This fixes drive-through cases where a mostly submerged car/building was expelled by the moving rim. Regression tests cover continued movement in both directions with actual loaded model dimensions, as well as shallow withdrawal and exactly-once scoring.

Still required for M2 acceptance: human validation of the foreground cutaway, a fuller material-specific audio mix, route tuning with human input, camera/landscape refinement, further set dressing and sustained real-phone performance testing. Stationary/consumed prop transforms are no longer rewritten each frame; active tipping/falling/rocking objects retain per-frame updates. New authored models bake their material colors into vertex colors, keeping each variant in one instanced draw batch. Chunking and device-specific performance work remain pending.
